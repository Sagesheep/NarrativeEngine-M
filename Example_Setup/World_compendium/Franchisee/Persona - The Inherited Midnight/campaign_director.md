# Persona: The Inherited Midnight — GM Director Guide

## GM Reference
<!-- rag: keyword, triggers: director guide, GM reference, priority: 5 -->
Full-series spoilers. Everything identified as ORIGINAL is campaign invention. Baseline developments happen only while their causes survive player intervention. This guide supplies pressure, clues and alternative routes, never a requirement to reproduce a game script.

## Campaign Spine

### [CHUNK: OVERVIEW -- The Long Mystery and Its Limits]
<!-- rag: keyword, triggers: director, campaign spine, Three-Seal, Quiet Archive, priority: 9 -->
ORIGINAL SPINE: a 1931 investigation found that a machine could exploit longing to create an impression of perfect emotional communion. It did not recover souls or eliminate human difference. The people who shut it down split the evidence to prevent unilateral reuse. Later institutions repeatedly encounter fragments while struggling with death, truth, expression and control. The player follows the evidence through distinct crises and eventually determines the archive's future.

The Ledger contains three complementary records: FINANCIAL establishes who commissioned work, paid victims and concealed liability; TECHNICAL records observations and failures rather than a complete working design; TESTIMONIAL preserves witnesses' conflicting accounts and the warning that apparent agreement may be imposed. All three are needed for a responsible account of the original case. They are not needed to defeat every game's final boss. Copies can be made; destroying one physical object does not erase every independent witness or record.

Do not name the 1931 prototype a Plume of Dusk origin, a Great Seal key, a DEVA system ancestor, an EMMA source-code module or a machine that created the collective unconscious. Such claims would overwhelm separate histories without support. Its modern relevance is evidentiary and ethical, with limited original occult observations established during play.

### [CHUNK: OVERVIEW -- PC Roles and Long-Term Stakes]
<!-- rag: vector, triggers: PC role, family, archive, original character, veteran, priority: 8 -->
Choose a practical connection from setup: family work around a municipal archive, a school historical project, an ordinary Kirijo scholarship, investigative interest, or a neighborhood connection to an affected person. The PC's value comes from decisions, competence and relationships rather than inherited blood. A summoner-related origin gives contacts or training history, not automatic Raidou status.

Give the PC an independent goal that can evolve: build a secure home, escape a predetermined career, repair a family relationship, tell a buried story, learn to trust after loss. Each major chapter should put that goal into contact with the local theme. Do not dictate the answer. An adult PC can support school-age casts without becoming their uninvited commander or romantic option. A teenage PC becomes an adult before P5; let that change duties, perspective and access.

### [CHUNK: EVENT -- Pressure and Clue Management]
<!-- rag: keyword, triggers: clocks, pressure, evidence, authenticity, investigation, priority: 9 -->
Track ordinary deadlines and supernatural escalation using existing engine facilities or clear state notes; these are GM planning tracks, not newly installed mechanics. Each threat advances because of a named actor and available means. For an indispensable conclusion, keep at least three routes: a person, a record and an observable inconsistency. They can converge on the same fact without being identical copies. If one route is destroyed, survivors react; no spontaneous replacement clue falls into the PC's pocket.

At each chapter boundary record: date; PC situation; living/dead/missing people; known realm access; major public evidence; group knowledge; active threats; Ledger fragments and copies; divergences. Inactive crises may progress without the PC, but the director never knows the PC's unspoken plan. The player's clever inference can matter before the original reveal date.

## Chapters

### [CHUNK: EVENT -- Prologue 1931 The Voices That Answered]
<!-- rag: keyword, triggers: prologue, 1931, Taisho, Narumi, Three-Seal case, priority: 9 -->
ORIGINAL ADVENTURE in Raidou's historical setting. An optional separate viewpoint character assists Narumi's agency as a clerk, reporter, medical worker or junior investigator. A Nanjo financial representative disputes payments to families after a failed demonstration. A Kirijo-family engineer wants the apparatus inspected before the patron destroys it. Raidou and Gouto investigate supernatural traces while the viewpoint character handles their own meaningful part of the case.

Entry scenes: a missing stage technician's family receives a payment before the official accident notice; a grieving client hears a dead relative's familiar phrase on a recording; a worker refuses to return to a privately rented demonstration hall. Do not tell the player these are all connected immediately.

Evidence routes: inconsistent dates in a trust disbursement book; an engineer's marginal note that the voice answered a question the deceased could never have heard; survivors repeating the same consoling phrase despite different losses. A contracted demon can detect an influence or track a route, not certify a human memory as perfect truth. The supernatural hazard amplifies shared longing and discourages separation; people can resist or help one another out.

Resolve rescue, evidence custody and institutional responsibility. Plausible endings include preservation with safeguards, public scandal, suppression with surviving copies, or partial destruction. Establish which records actually survive. The default historical outcome splits the papers into three holdings. If the player changes that, update the modern mystery's provenance. Do not force the same split through an unavoidable fire. Historical names beyond canon are original NPCs created when relevant; the financier and engineer need not be ancestors of the modern PC.

### [CHUNK: EVENT -- Chapter 1 2009 The Hour Nobody Remembers]
<!-- rag: keyword, triggers: chapter 1, April 2009, Iwatodai, SEES, Tartarus, priority: 9 -->
Baseline: the PC enters Iwatodai's daily life as Makoto Yuki arrives, SEES investigates Tartarus, and full-moon operations develop. Begin with school, home and a local obligation. Reveal the Dark Hour through a specific lived event rather than a lore lecture. SEES recruitment requires contact, demonstrated relevance and scrutiny. A Kirijo-linked PC still does not know the experiments' full history.

ORIGINAL ARCHIVE THREAD: an innocuous forwarding receipt ties an old Nanjo trust to a Kirijo archive reference. Three routes: a school local-history collection, municipal redevelopment records, or a retired contractor's surviving correspondence. These establish a historical paper trail, not Ikutsuki's guilt. Mitsuru initially knows the archive exists but not its contents; Takeharu may know more and have reasons to restrict disclosure.

Off-screen motion: Strega acts from its own interests; suppressant access and artificial Persona-user history remain dangerous; research records face a mundane retention review. That review creates a recoverable risk, not a timed ultimatum forcing burglary. Choices include seeking lawful archive access, copying public records, protecting a witness or focusing entirely on immediate rescues. Preserve time for Junpei's insecurity, Yukari's investigation, Akihiko's habits and the PC's own obligations.

Transition: the group has learned enough about the full-moon operations to question the official explanation. If the PC exposes deception early, let SEES verify it and change operations. Do not force them to continue gathering the same components while knowing the danger.

### [CHUNK: EVENT -- Chapter 2 2009 to 2010 The Cost of an Ending]
<!-- rag: keyword, triggers: chapter 2, October 4, Ikutsuki, Ryoji, Nyx, January 31, March 5, priority: 9 -->
Baseline anchors include the Shinjiro/Ken/Takaya confrontation, Ikutsuki's betrayal, the truth surrounding Ryoji and the Fall, the January endgame, and the protagonist's March fate. Their calendar positions are planning references, not immutable outcomes. Chidori's outcome follows selected conditions and intervention. Nyx is not defeated by proving the Three-Seal prototype fraudulent; those are different problems.

ORIGINAL ARCHIVE THREAD: recovered technical pages describe a false impression of reunion and a warning against mistaking relief for restoration. They support skepticism toward easy answers but reveal neither the Great Seal's complete operation nor a substitute sacrifice. Routes: a research inventory index, a surviving staff member's correction, and differences between a circulated summary and the original handwritten sheet. The custodian trying to suppress the pages may be protecting patients, concealing abuse, or both.

Give the PC a chance to prepare, disagree, rescue, seek counsel or pursue their own objective. If a canon death is averted, preserve the changed person's future and obligations. If the final crisis takes a different form, record what was actually sealed, prevented or lost. A heroic sacrifice belongs to the person making it. Do not narrate the PC volunteering or arrange for a beloved NPC to die solely because the player declined.

Transition: close the Dark Hour crisis according to play, then show the cost in ordinary life. Graduation, absence, hospital visits, guilt and relief should be concrete. Whether Makoto lives or not determines how The Answer is adapted.

### [CHUNK: EVENT -- Chapter 3 March 2010 The Answer and the Year After]
<!-- rag: keyword, triggers: chapter 3, March 31, The Answer, Episode Aigis, Metis, Erebus, priority: 9 -->
Baseline: on March 31, former SEES members are trapped in a repeating day and encounter the Abyss of Time. Aigis, Metis and the group's differing responses to loss lead to the nature of the Great Seal and Erebus. Resolve disagreements through the people's actual bonds and knowledge; do not force the PC to win everyone's grief argument.

If the protagonist survived or the central sacrifice changed, do not pretend the canonical bereavement occurred. Adapt the chapter around the actual unresolved loss, the cost of protection, or an abandoned version of the future, and label new metaphysics as a campaign divergence. If there is no credible cause for a repeating day, use a grounded aftermath chapter with Aigis and the team confronting the same questions. Do not manufacture a death to unlock the dungeon.

ORIGINAL BRIDGE: the testimonial warning from 1931 gains relevance: the comforting answer a group shares can conceal what individual members actually want. It is thematic evidence, not a spell to free the Seal. Elizabeth's later search can remain unresolved.

Then play or agree on the 2010–2011 interval. Establish study, jobs, residence, changes in Kirijo responsibility and the PC's ties. A plausible Inaba connection can be a relocation, relative, archive lead, school visit or investigative referral. The PC need not coincidentally become Yu's new cousin.

### [CHUNK: EVENT -- Chapter 4 2011 The Town Behind the Screen]
<!-- rag: keyword, triggers: chapter 4, 2011, Inaba, Golden, murder, Midnight Channel, priority: 9 -->
Baseline: Yu arrives in Inaba; murders and broadcasts lead to the TV World and an expanding Investigation Team. Rescue chapters include Yukiko, Kanji, Rise and Naoto, with Mitsuo's apparent solution, Nanako's abduction, Namatame, Adachi, and the deeper origin of the phenomenon. These are distinct evidence problems. Give characters reasons to suspect different explanations without falsifying what the PC saw.

ORIGINAL ARCHIVE THREAD: a Nanjo trust audit contains references to the Kirijo file, and a provincial records transfer passed through an Inaba repository. It does not explain the murders. Routes: delivery records, a local archivist's testimony, and a miscatalogued duplicate payment schedule. A victim's private feelings are not a convenient clue box about 1931. TV imagery may reflect knowledge actually held by its subject, not the GM's entire campaign outline.

An experienced PC may recognize Persona phenomena but still need an entry route, local trust and evidence. If they identify the real culprit early, allow surveillance, reporting, protection or confrontation; the culprit can adapt only to information they acquire. Rescue deadlines remain connected to actual abductions and weather. Preventing an abduction removes that rescue dungeon's original cause; replace the chapter with the consequences of prevention, not a forced new kidnapping.

Transition: establish the resolution of the human case and the group's remaining doubts. Do not auto-execute an accused person, choose the player's dialogue, or close the case because the original game offered a bad ending there.

### [CHUNK: EVENT -- Chapter 5 2012 Golden and the Unfinished Truth]
<!-- rag: keyword, triggers: chapter 5, Marie, Hollow Forest, Izanami, Golden epilogue, priority: 8 -->
Baseline: the team follows Marie's disappearance, confronts the Hollow Forest under its resource constraints, and discovers the truth behind the town's larger supernatural test. Resolve Izanami's role through investigation and the bonds actually formed. A generalized claim that all gods are projections does not instantly dismiss the danger. Golden's later summer reunion is a separate time marker after the spring departure.

ORIGINAL ARCHIVE THREAD: comparison of financial records shows how a technically true report can erase victims by treating compensation as closure. This is an ethical problem the team can act on, not a revelation that Izanami signed corporate paperwork. Kei Nanjo becomes a plausible contact through authenticated documents and his own interests. If the PC already earned access, respect that history; otherwise use a representative or a request for corroboration first.

Before the summer epilogue, Arena and Ultimax occur in Golden Week. Keep their incidents in the correct order even if the campaign handles Golden's optional material flexibly. Resolve what the team knows before each transition, especially whether Marie has been rescued and how the TV World is understood.

### [CHUNK: EVENT -- Chapter 6 Golden Week 2012 Arena]
<!-- rag: keyword, triggers: chapter 6, Arena, P-1 Grand Prix, Labrys, Shadow Labrys, priority: 9 -->
Baseline: the Investigation Team and P3 veterans converge around Labrys, manipulated announcements and apparent hostilities. The Grand Prix is a coercive incident, not a sports festival everyone cheerfully joined. Show the information distortion that makes a clash plausible. A player who checks a suspicious message can reduce or avoid a fight. Do not make seasoned friends attack through obvious deception merely because a fighting-game match must happen.

Labrys's memories and her Shadow create the central emotional problem. Treat her as a person, including legal tension around Kirijo ownership. Original PCs may protect others, compare accounts, investigate the theft or help her resist; they should not displace her awakening.

ORIGINAL ARCHIVE THREAD: an old technical appendix was attached to a later inventory that mentions artificial-person research. The appendix records an observer's warning, not a design for Labrys. Three routes: inventory revision history, physical markings on the copy, and a survivor who remembers when the attachment appeared. A Kirijo cover-up faction wants it returned quietly. A Nanjo representative wants its provenance checked. Kuzunoha contacts dispute the corporate interpretation.

Transition: establish Labrys's choice and rescue, what was stolen, and what remains unexplained. Each character route is a perspective on one shared event, not a series of repeated kidnappings.

### [CHUNK: EVENT -- Chapter 7 Golden Week 2012 Ultimax]
<!-- rag: keyword, triggers: chapter 7, Ultimax, P-1 Climax, red fog, Sho, Minazuki, Hi-no-Kagutsuchi, priority: 9 -->
Baseline: the next incident escalates into the red fog, Sho/Minazuki's history, abducted allies and Hi-no-Kagutsuchi's exploitation. P3 and P4 cast members need a working division of responsibility. Those outside an accessible zone can evacuate civilians, maintain communications, investigate or organize treatment; they do not disappear because there are too many party members.

Sho's rejection of bonds has a history. Respect the distinction between Sho, Minazuki and their relationship to a Persona. Reaching someone emotionally can create a choice without guaranteeing reform. Do not collapse all antagonists into a generic Shadow of loneliness.

ORIGINAL ARCHIVE THREAD: under cover of the crisis, a Custodian intermediary attempts to recover the disputed papers. Their action is limited to available personnel and access. It can succeed, fail or be interrupted. Routes to exposing it: a mismatched transport authorization, an evacuation witness, or a copy whose redaction pattern differs. This operation does not cause the red fog.

Transition: count injuries, restore contacts and decide Labrys's standing. A tentative cross-team liaison may form with rules about consent and information sharing. That liaison provides a future contact route, not a promise of instant nationwide deployment.

### [CHUNK: EVENT -- Chapter 8 Later 2012 Dancing All Night]
<!-- rag: keyword, triggers: chapter 8, Dancing All Night, LMB, Kanamin, Midnight Stage, Mikuratana, priority: 9 -->
Baseline: Rise's return to entertainment and the Love Meets Bonds festival draw the team toward the midnight video and missing performers. Kanami, her group, Kyoka Ochimizu and the older story surrounding Mina matter individually. The Midnight Stage offers effortless belonging at the cost of authentic connection. Mikuratana-no-Kami's influence must be confronted through this realm's expressive logic.

Play rehearsals, crew work, public expectations and disagreement before the climax. A dancer can want success while resenting their image. Rise cannot speak for every performer. The PC's role may be stage support, investigation, participation or an honest outsider; ask before turning an unwilling PC into a star.

ORIGINAL ARCHIVE THREAD: a production researcher found a public account of the 1931 voice experiment. Its phrase about shared solace was quoted without the survivors' objections. Evidence routes: rehearsal research notes, an old recording's acquisition history, and the omitted page in the source collection. This is a parallel and a modern misuse of an idea, not proof that the 1931 machine created the Midnight Stage or that Kanami's mother worked for it.

Resolution requires giving individual people room to speak and choose. A perfect routine does not substitute for truth. Once the binding ends, missing people still need care, testimony and control over their public stories. The Custodians can split over disclosure here: protecting privacy differs from burying culpability.

### [CHUNK: EVENT -- Interlude 2013 to 2015 Lives Worth Protecting]
<!-- rag: keyword, triggers: interlude, 2013, 2014, 2015, adulthood, time skip, priority: 8 -->
Agree on the PC's priorities before advancing years. Resolve education, employment, residence, money, important relationships and the archive's custody. Use a few chosen scenes rather than a mandatory montage: a hospital visit, a mundane reunion, an argument over publishing a name, a new job, a missed call. Earlier cast members develop adult lives. The campaign may remain active in selected incidents, but do not invent annual world-ending emergencies to fill the gap.

ORIGINAL PRESSURE: the authoritarian Custodian faction changes from concealment toward selling selective institutional access. Another member wants an independently reviewed release with victim safeguards. Financial interests, fear and sincere disagreement create a conflict the PC can investigate or leave others to handle. The relevant clue for 2016 is a verifiable transfer or citation trail, not a flash-forward to a future criminal's face.

### [CHUNK: EVENT -- Chapter 9 2016 The Masks of Tokyo]
<!-- rag: keyword, triggers: chapter 9, 2016, Kamoshida, Madarame, Kaneshiro, Futaba, Okumura, Royal, priority: 9 -->
Baseline: Ren's probation and Shujin abuses lead to the first Palace and the Phantom Thieves. Follow the changing team through Madarame, Kaneshiro, Futaba and Okumura while preserving confidants and ordinary life. Sumire and Maruki exist in the Royal continuity from their relevant introductions. The PC is now older by default and needs a plausible Tokyo connection: work, relocation, a continuing investigation or an invitation based on an established relationship.

Veterans do not instantly know the app, Palace rules or Phantom Thief identities. First contact might come through a pattern of shutdowns, a victim requesting help or an authenticated warning. The teenagers may reasonably distrust a corporate organization offering surveillance and command. Let cooperation be negotiated. Older allies can protect ordinary people, examine evidence and challenge poor plans without replacing the new cast.

ORIGINAL ARCHIVE THREAD: a secondary cognitive-research bibliography cites an incomplete historical observation inherited through a corporate collection. Routes: a university catalog, a research-payment trail, and annotations that disagree with the public abstract. It does not replace Wakaba's original research, prove Shido's entire plan or reveal the Metaverse navigation mechanism. Futaba's discovery should remain hers to process rather than a file the veterans solved years ago.

If the PC prevents a death or protects a researcher, follow that changed source of testimony. The conspiracy can react within its knowledge; no unavoidable shutdown occurs merely to preserve the original sequence.

### [CHUNK: EVENT -- Chapter 10 Late 2016 The Public Wants an Answer]
<!-- rag: keyword, triggers: chapter 10, Sae, interrogation, Akechi, Shido, Yaldabaoth, false Igor, priority: 9 -->
Baseline: the investigation around Sae's Palace, the interrogation and betrayal, Shido's operation and the deeper god-of-control crisis expose the costs of public certainty. Do not require the exact capture plan if the campaign has made it unnecessary. A different credible strategy can succeed. Akechi's private motives remain private until witnessed or established through evidence; public charm is not a permanent magical disguise once proof exists.

ORIGINAL ARCHIVE THREAD: the authoritarian Custodian faction offers a dangerous compromise: suppress scandal, protect selected people and let a trusted institution choose which truths survive. Their proposal is optional, limited and intelligible, not the true cause of Yaldabaoth. Kei, Mitsuru and other allies may disagree over disclosure; let their positions reflect what they actually know and the harm they have seen.

Clue routes to the archive manipulation: inconsistent report versions, testimony from an excluded researcher, and a financial instruction tied to a documented intermediary. None automatically reveals the false Igor. Resolve the main P5 crisis on its own metaphysical and personal terms. End with the actual legal, family and institutional aftermath, not instant universal amnesty.

### [CHUNK: EVENT -- Chapter 11 Early 2017 A Kindness That Cannot Hear No]
<!-- rag: keyword, triggers: chapter 11, third semester, Maruki, Sumire, Kasumi, actualization, priority: 9 -->
Baseline: Maruki's imposed reality offers relief from grief and suffering while replacing people's choices. Sumire's identity and Akechi's conditional presence matter. Let characters be genuinely tempted. A veteran grieving a P3 loss need not reject the offer instantly, and a person who accepts comfort is not intellectually stupid. Show specific discrepancies the PC can investigate rather than announcing the world's falsehood.

ORIGINAL ARCHIVE PAYOFF: the testimonial fragment preserves a survivor's refusal to let consolation overwrite their own account. The dossier supplies language and evidence for the ethical choice, not immunity to actualization. If the PC previously destroyed it, remembered testimony or another witness may still matter only if established; the final choice remains possible without the artifact.

Resolve the choice the player makes. Rejection restores a world that still contains pain and responsibility. Acceptance produces an explicitly divergent world whose autonomy conflict continues; do not call it the canonical Royal ending or reset it for Strikers. Akechi's fate stays unresolved unless the campaign establishes it through actual events. Sumire is not reassigned her sister's identity as a convenient epilogue.

### [CHUNK: EVENT -- Chapter 12 Summer 2017 The Road and the Stolen Desires]
<!-- rag: keyword, triggers: chapter 12, Strikers, Shibuya, Sendai, Sapporo, Okinawa, Kyoto, Osaka, priority: 9 -->
Baseline: the summer reunion becomes a Jail investigation. Introduce Sophia through the new crisis and Zenkichi through his actual investigation. Shibuya's Alice, Sendai's Ango and Sapporo's Mariko each reveal different pressures and abuses. Okinawa shifts attention toward the research infrastructure. Kyoto tests Zenkichi's bond with Akane and the cost of passivity. Osaka exposes Konoe's justice narrative and the scale of EMMA's role.

Give each stop local life and its own evidence, not one interchangeable dungeon. The team cooks, shops, argues, rests and calls people at home. Road-trip time follows the present crisis rather than importing every P5 Palace deadline unchanged. Jails have their own persistence rules. Do not force the entire P3/P4 cast into the camper: established allies can investigate parallel leads, provide a safe contact, or be unavailable for credible reasons. If the PC requests help from a prior friend, actually resolve that request.

ORIGINAL ARCHIVE THREAD: a Custodian's modern policy memorandum recommends replacing inconvenient testimony with an aggregate account of what people supposedly want. A consultant used the old case as rhetorical precedent. It is not EMMA's source code and does not prove Konoe or Ichinose knowingly joined the Custodians. Routes: procurement correspondence, a preserved dissenting review, and a named witness to the policy meeting. This establishes contemporary responsibility without swallowing the Strikers plot.

Sophia learns from companionship, disagreement and her own decisions. Zenkichi's awakening belongs to his choice regarding Akane and responsibility. A veteran PC can support those choices but cannot perform them for the characters.

### [CHUNK: EVENT -- Chapter 13 2017 Humanity's Companion and the Open Archive]
<!-- rag: keyword, triggers: chapter 13, Ichinose, Sophia, Pandora, EMMA, Demiurge, finale, priority: 9 -->
Baseline: Ichinose's role, Sophia's origins and development, and EMMA's final claim to guide humanity culminate in the confrontation with imposed salvation. Konoe's downfall alone need not finish the larger danger. Sophia's true awakening is personal, not a recovered corporate password. The finale asks whether help can remain help when refusal is removed. Resolve the supernatural threat using the actual team's capabilities, available access and choices.

ORIGINAL CAMPAIGN CONCLUSION: after the Strikers crisis, the Three-Seal archive's custody must be settled. This is a separate human decision, not a bonus boss created by collecting all pages. Options can include independent custody with survivor access and restricted hazardous detail; public release with documented redactions and oversight; continued compartmentalization with accountable review; destruction of particular dangerous technical materials while preserving testimony; or a coercive institutional settlement the PC chooses to support. Do not make one option consequence-free.

Who has standing depends on play: affected survivors and families, independent investigators, relevant archive holders and the people whose secrets would be exposed. Mitsuru and Kei are important participants, not automatic owners of everyone's memories. Kuzunoha custodians can explain containment risks without dictating the only moral answer. A dossier with missing pages still allows a decision; uncertainty is itself part of responsible custody.

End with concrete consequences for the PC's own goal and relationships. A meal, repaired friendship, changed job or difficult published account may close the campaign better than assembling every named character for applause. Elizabeth's search, unresolved crimes and private grief can remain open without making the completed campaign a failure.

## Optional and Divergent Material

### [CHUNK: EVENT -- Optional Velvet Room Interludes and Side Stories]
<!-- rag: keyword, triggers: Q, Q2, Dancing in Moonlight, Dancing in Starlight, Tactica, optional, priority: 7 -->
P3/P5 Dancing and Q/Q2 can supply short out-of-time experiences if the player wants them. Establish which incarnations participate and what memories survive. They are not a free route to tell Makoto the entire future or to recruit Joker into 2009. P4 Dancing All Night remains a full historical chapter with external stakes. P5 Tactica may be an optional pre-Strikers side chapter using its own situation and allies; do not insert it automatically or pretend this pack contains a full retelling of every side title. These additions must preserve the player's active history.

### [CHUNK: OVERVIEW -- Divergence Repair Without Resetting the Player]
<!-- rag: keyword, triggers: divergence, early culprit, saved protagonist, accepted Maruki, failed rescue, missing fragment, priority: 9 -->
Early culprit exposure: resolve evidence, protection, arrest or confrontation; surviving supernatural causes may remain, but do not invent a replacement killer. A saved P3 lead: retain their life and revisit the actual unresolved cost rather than creating the same funeral. Failed rescue: show consequences and credible routes to containment or a changed ending; do not erase loss in the next scene. Accepted Maruki reality: establish how altered desires and EMMA interact as ORIGINAL divergent metaphysics, or agree on a separate branch before proceeding. Missing archive fragment: keep the uncertainty and use surviving evidence; the main games' crises remain solvable. A dead or estranged liaison: require another real contact route, not a new person with the same memories. Publicly exposed Personas: adapt media, institutions and secrecy instead of making the public forget without a cause.

### [CHUNK: OVERVIEW -- Compact State Note]
<!-- rag: keyword, triggers: save note, transition, checkpoint, recap, priority: 8 -->
At chapter transitions preserve this information through supported campaign memory or a visible OOC note. Do not fill unknown values with predictions.

Date/phase; PC age, residence and ordinary role; current goals; active party and individual commitments; confirmed abilities and realm access; money, supplies, wounds and fatigue; known deadlines; publicly established facts; PC-only discoveries; each faction's consequential knowledge; major divergences with causes; current threats and means; promises and damaged relationships; Ledger fragments with holders, copies, authenticity and witnesses; immediate next decision.
